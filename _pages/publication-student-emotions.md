## Abstract

Student emotions shape learning by influencing attention, engagement, and self-regulation. Virtual field trips (VFTs) in desktop virtual reality can elicit affect, yet the emotion-learning relationship in construction VFTs remains understudied. This paper tests whether facial-expression-derived emotion metrics during a drywall-focused, instructor-guided VFT relate to learning gains. Webcam video was analyzed with a computer-vision pipeline (S3FD face detection and EmoNet classification) to label five expressions (neutral, happy, surprise, fear, sad). We computed expression-duration proportions and an emotion switch rate and related them to pre-/post-test improvement. After a face-visibility quality filter, 16 participants were analyzed. Negative expression proportion (fear + sad) correlated with learning gains (r = 0.532, p = 0.034, R² = 0.283), as did switch rate (r = 0.506, p = 0.046, R² = 0.256), while neutral proportion showed a nearly significant marginal negative association (r = −0.484, p = 0.058, f² = 0.306).

**Keywords:** Virtual field trips; construction education; affective computing; facial expression recognition; computer vision
